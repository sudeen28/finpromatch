What forensic accounting covers

Forensic accounting sits at the intersection of accounting and investigation. A forensic accountant in Nigeria typically examines financial records to identify irregularities, trace the movement of funds, quantify losses from suspected fraud, and, where needed, prepare findings that can withstand scrutiny in a legal or disciplinary process. This differs meaningfully from a standard audit, which checks for material misstatement rather than actively investigating for wrongdoing.

Common situations that call for a forensic accountant

Businesses typically reach out when they suspect internal fraud, such as a staff member manipulating records or diverting funds; when a partner or shareholder dispute requires an independent financial review; when due diligence on an acquisition uncovers something that doesn't add up; or when a matter is already headed toward litigation and needs a financial expert's findings to support it. Each of these calls for a slightly different mix of skills, which is exactly why matching matters more than simply finding "an accountant."

What a forensic accounting engagement typically involves

Depending on the case, a forensic accountant may reconstruct incomplete or altered records, interview staff alongside management or legal counsel, analyze transaction patterns for anomalies, and prepare a formal report setting out findings and methodology. The scope is usually agreed upfront based on what you're trying to establish, whether that's the size of a loss, how it happened, or who was involved.
