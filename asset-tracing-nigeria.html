What asset tracing involves

Asset tracing follows the movement of money and property, often across multiple accounts, entities, or jurisdictions, to establish where funds or assets actually ended up. It draws on many of the same skills as forensic accounting but is specifically focused on locating and documenting assets rather than examining records for irregularities in isolation. The output is often used to support recovery action, a legal claim, or a settlement negotiation.

When businesses and individuals need this service

Common situations include suspected diversion of company funds by a director or employee, tracing assets in a matrimonial or partnership dispute, supporting a debt recovery effort where a debtor's assets aren't where they should be, and due diligence work ahead of litigation or a major transaction. Each scenario calls for a slightly different investigative approach and evidentiary standard.

What the process typically looks like

An asset tracing engagement usually starts with gathering everything already known about the individual or entity involved, followed by a structured review of financial records, corporate filings, and property or transaction history. Findings are documented in a way that can support whatever comes next, whether that's a recovery claim, a legal filing, or a negotiation, so the methodology behind the work matters as much as the conclusions themselves.
