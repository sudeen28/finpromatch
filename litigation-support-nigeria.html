What litigation support accountants actually do

Litigation support work typically involves quantifying financial loss or damages, reviewing the opposing party's financial claims and evidence, preparing clear financial reports for legal counsel, and in some cases, providing formal expert witness testimony. It requires both strong technical accounting skills and the ability to communicate findings in a way that's understandable outside a purely financial context, which is a genuinely different skill set from routine bookkeeping or audit work.

Where this comes up

Businesses and individuals typically need litigation support during shareholder or partnership disputes, contract disputes involving financial claims, matrimonial proceedings with significant business or asset value at stake, and professional negligence or breach-of-duty claims with a financial dimension. Legal counsel often requests this support directly, but the underlying need is the same: a financial expert whose analysis and testimony can stand up to cross-examination.

Working alongside your legal team

A litigation support accountant typically works in close coordination with your lawyers rather than independently, since the financial analysis needs to align with the legal strategy and the specific points in dispute. That usually means early conversations about what needs to be established, followed by document review, analysis, and a report structured around the questions the case actually turns on.
